## Understanding the Core Combat Physics

The foundation of *Gladio Mori* revolves around realistic biomechanics and weapon mass. Your character does not deal damage simply because an attack animation is playing; the weapon blade must physically make contact with adequate velocity to cleave flesh, crack bone, or pierce vital organs.

Every attack draws from your stamina bar and relies heavily on your character's rotational momentum. If you swing a greatsword while retreating, the relative velocity drops significantly, resulting in a glancing blow that barely chips armor. Conversely, stepping into your swing and rotating your torso amplifies kinetic force, turning a basic horizontal slash into an instantaneous kill.

| Combat Mechanic | How It Functions | Tactical Importance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Edge Alignment** | Blade orientation relative to the target's surface | Prevents flat-side glancing blows; ensures maximum cutting depth |
| **Rotational Momentum** | Adding torso and footwork rotation to swings | Increases kinetic impact, turning standard strikes into lethal decapitations |
| **Anatomical Hitboxes** | Damage calculated based on biological targets (neck, heart, organs) | Bypasses hitpoint attrition by allowing one-hit lethal strikes |
| **Physics-Based Parrying** | Blade-on-blade collision detection | Deflects incoming kinetic force without scripted input locks |

Understanding these underlying systems changes your approach entirely. Rather than mashing buttons, you must view every encounter as a high-stakes duel where timing, spacing, and angle of approach dictate the victor.

## Custom Animation and Move Creation Basics

One of the standout systems in *Gladio Mori* is the animation editor, enabling players to choreograph, edit, and fine-tune their custom attacks. While downloading community-created movesets is popular, learning how the editor balances frames, speed, and recovery will prevent you from bringing unviable moves into ranked duels.

Every custom move consists of three distinct phases: wind-up, strike execution, and recovery. If you make your wind-up too fast, the game will penalize the strike's kinetic energy; if you neglect recovery posture, your gladiator will remain completely vulnerable to counter-thrusts.

| Animation Phase | Key Focus | Common Beginner Mistake | Ideal Configuration |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Phase 1: Wind-Up** | Gathering momentum and shifting center of mass | Telegraphed wind-ups that take more than 0.5s | Compact coil of the torso with a forward lead step |
| **Phase 2: Execution** | Blade velocity and forward hip drive | Over-rotating past the target line | Snapping the blade flat through target center mass |
| **Phase 3: Recovery** | Returning weapon to defensive guard | Leaving limbs extended after the hit window | Pulling the elbows tight into an immediate high or low guard |

When building your initial moveset, focus on economy of motion. Flowery, acrobatic spins look impressive in practice mode, but player experience confirms that disciplined, linear thrusts and short-arc snaps win tournaments.

## Defensive Mastery: Footwork, Spacing, and Parrying

Offense gets the glory, but flawless defense keeps you alive across multi-round bouts. In *Gladio Mori*, turtling behind a block is rarely a permanent solution because heavy blows can stagger your posture or knock your weapon aside. True defense relies on controlling the spacing (often referred to as *spacing* or *footsies* in combat games).

Mastering the following defensive techniques will dramatically reduce early deaths in the arena:

*   **Slipping the Jab:** Stepping slightly off-line diagonally forward when an opponent initiates a linear thrust, opening an immediate angle to target their exposed neck.
*   **Active Deflection:** Striking into the opponent's incoming weapon path with your own crossguard or blade forte (strong section near the hilt) rather than remaining passive.
*   **Zone Retreat:** Dropping your guard into a sprinting stance for a split second to pull your torso just outside the weapon tip's maximum reach, then stepping back in while they recover.
*   **Chamber Parries:** Starting an identical mirroring strike at the moment an opponent attacks, meeting their blade mid-flight while your weapon continues onward to strike.

By relying on distance rather than physical weapon blocks, you conserve precious stamina. When an enemy exhausts their energy missing you, a single calculated riposte will end the duel.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why do my attacks glance off opponents without dealing lethal damage?
Glancing blows occur when your weapon lacks sufficient velocity upon impact or your edge alignment is crooked. Make sure you are rotating your torso into the attack and striking with the sweet spot (upper third) of your blade rather than hitting with the flat side or near the crossguard.

### How do I stop getting one-shotted in the opening seconds of a match?
Never run headfirst into an opponent during the opening clash. Most early deaths happen because players charge into an extended spear or longsword thrust. Approach at a slight angle, bait their first swing by briefly entering and exiting their weapon reach, and strike while they are trapped in recovery animation frames.

### Can I be competitive using only default movesets in this gladiomori beginner guide?
Yes. Default movesets are balanced with reliable recovery frames, neutral postures, and safe hitboxes. While custom moves allow personal expression and unexpected angles, solid fundamentals, spacing, and timing with default attacks will outplay poorly optimized custom setups every time.

### How does stamina impact weapon damage in Gladio Mori?
When your stamina dips into the red threshold, your character loses swing speed and body stability. Because kinetic impact damage depends directly on blade velocity, low stamina results in sluggish strikes that glance harmlessly off your opponent's body or fail to penetrate armor.
