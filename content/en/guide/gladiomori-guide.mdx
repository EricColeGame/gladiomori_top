## Core Combat Mechanics: How Damage and Physics Work

Unlike conventional fighting games relying on static frame data and canned hit animations, combat in this arena is fully physics-simulated. Weapon momentum, mass, angle of entry, and velocity dictate whether a strike bounces harmlessly off armor or cleaves clean through flesh.

When an attack lands, the game calculates kinetic energy based on your character's rotational torque and forward momentum. A stationary thrust deals minimal impact, whereas a lunge utilizing your full body weight creates fatal penetration depth. Community reports and competitive player testing reveal that even grazing hits to vital zones can cause rapid bleed-out, severely limiting your opponent's stamina and movement speed before finishing them off.

### Damage Zones and Lethality Matrix

To optimize your offense, you must target the anatomical weak points that yield instant knockouts or fatal hemorrhage.

Understanding these anatomical hitboxes allows you to tailor your stances. If your adversary keeps a tight guard protecting their chest, sweeping low toward the knee can force their weapon downward, leaving their head completely unprotected for a follow-up strike.

### Detailed Weapon Overview

#### Longsword: The Gold Standard
The two-handed longsword provides the most balanced toolkit. Its crossguard offers exceptional defensive coverage, while its dual cutting edges allow for deceptive trajectory shifts mid-swing. It serves as the benchmark weapon for anyone following this **gladiomori guide** to learn fundamental spacing.

#### Rapier: Surgical Precision
Rapiers sacrifice horizontal slashing capability for unmatched thrust velocity. While a rapier cannot cleave limbs, its tip travels faster than any other weapon point in the game. Stabbing directly into the eye socket or throat produces instantaneous kills before the opponent's swing completes its arc.

#### Greatsword / Zweihänder: Momentum Mastery
Wielding heavy weapons requires managing deadweight. If you whiff a horizontal sweep with a greatsword, your recovery animation leaves you completely open. However, continuous half-swording and momentum-chaining can break through any defensive guard, literally knocking smaller weapons out of an opponent's hands.

## Step-by-Step Tactical Gameplay Strategy

Surviving high-tier matches requires methodical neutral play. Winning consistently across various arena layouts relies on strict tactical flow. This practical walkthrough will refine your combat approach:

### The Tactical Engagement Protocol

| Phase | Core Objective | Key Mechanical Action | Risk Assessment |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Phase 1: Neutral Spacing** | Measure range & bait whiffs | Micro-stepping just outside strike range | Low (Maintain defensive buffer) |
| **Phase 2: Guard Manipulation** | Force an overcommitment | Feint high or tap their blade to force parry | Moderate (Vulnerable to quick thrusts) |
| **Phase 3: Kinetic Strike** | Deliver maximum lethal damage | Step into the attack with body rotation | High (Committed attack state) |
| **Phase 4: Reset or Execute** | Confirm kill or establish safety | Immediate back-dash or circular strafe | Moderate (Watch for counter-swings) |

### Step 1: Establish Distance (The Neutral Game)
Never rush straight into the center of the pit. Circle smoothly, testing your opponent's reaction by stepping into their perceived strike boundary and immediately stepping out. Watch how they react—nervous fighters will swing prematurely, leaving their weapon caught in recovery.

### Step 2: Control the Centerline
In medieval fencing theory, controlling the centerline between two combatants dictates who controls the bout. Keep your weapon's point aimed at the enemy's face. By occupying the centerline, any direct attack they throw must travel around your guard, giving you a shorter, straighter path to hit them first.

### Step 3: Capitalize on Momentum Redirection
When your weapon connects with enemy steel, do not pull directly backward. Slide your blade along theirs—known as winding—to redirect their point away from your body while sliding your own blade directly toward their hands or collarbone.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### How do I stop missing my thrusts in Gladio Mori?
Thrust precision depends heavily on character alignment and momentum. Ensure your crosshair remains locked onto the opponent's chest or head throughout the entire release animation. Additionally, avoid turning your camera wildly during a lunge, as physics torque can whip the blade tip off course, reducing both accuracy and impact force.

### Can custom animations be shared between players?
Yes, the community actively shares custom moveset codes and exported animation files. You can import community-designed moves directly into your move library, test them against training dummies, and tweak their timing curves to fit your specific reaction speeds and weapon preferences.

### What is the best weapon for beginners in this gladiomori guide?
The standard Longsword is universally recommended for new players. It offers an ideal middle ground between defensive blade surface area, generous reach, and forgiving recovery speeds. It allows you to practice cuts, thrusts, and binds without the severe recovery penalties of heavier polearms.

### Why do some hits deal no damage even when they touch the enemy?
Because damage is physics-calculated rather than trigger-based, a blade that touches an opponent without sufficient kinetic energy or velocity will simply slide or bounce off without penetrating. Strikes delivered at the very tail-end of a swing's arc or from an awkward, cramped angle often lack the force required to break skin or pierce armor.
