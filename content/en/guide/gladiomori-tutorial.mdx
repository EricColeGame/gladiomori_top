## Core Combat Mechanics: How Strikes and Guards Work

At its heart, the combat system relies on a geometric clock-face layout mapped directly across your keyboard or controller. Community reports highlight that the default keyboard binds are visually mapped to match strike trajectories. 

The center key (`[K]`) functions as your central thrust, while surrounding keys (`[U]`, `[I]`, `[O]`, `[J]`, `[L]`, and `[,]`) represent strikes coming from surrounding compass angles.

```
Key Layout Concept:
[U] (Top-Left)     [ I ] (High/12:00)     [O] (Special 2)
[J] (Left/9:00)    [K]   (Thrust/Stab)    [L] (Right/3:00)
                   [,]   (Low/Crouch)
```

### The "Attack as a Guard" System

In *Gladio Mori*, you rarely press a dedicated block button. Instead, **every attack ends in a guard stance**. When you hold an attack key, your gladiator maintains that final frame as an active guard. 

*   **Holding Stances:** If you execute a low sweep and hold the key, your fighter remains crouched, shielding their lower body. You can then release other attacks straight from that crouched position.
*   **Release Priority:** When chaining attacks out of a held position, releasing the secondary key returns you to your primary held guard.
*   **Opposing Coverage:** Many strikes place your weapon or shield on the opposite side of your swing, requiring you to think ahead about where your opponent's blade will land next.

## Weapon Masterclass: Sword and Shield

The Sword and Shield loadout represents the most forgiving kit for new fighters. The shield provides generous hitboxes that intercept incoming thrusts and horizontal swings, provided your directional stance matches the incoming angle.

### Clock Angles & Defensive Guard Properties

Player experience shows that slashes can be tracked like hours on a clock face. Every slash rotates your shield into a dedicated protective angle while your sword covers the strike vector.

| Input Key | Direction / Clock Position | Resulting Guard | Tactical Application |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`[U]` (Special 1)** | 10:30 (High-Left) | High-Left Guard | Closes down overhead swings from right-handed opponents. |
| **`[J]` (Left Attack)** | 10:30 (Mid-Left) | Left Guard | Standard horizontal parry while advancing forward. |
| **`[I]` (High Attack)** | 12:00 (Direct Vertical) | Left Guard | Punishes crouching rivals while shielding your upper torso. |
| **`[L]` (Right Attack)** | 3:00 (Mid-Right) | High Guard | Swings across your body; leaves lower body open. |
| **`[,]` (Low Attack)** | 5:00 (Low Sweep) | High Guard (Crouched) | Sweeps low legs while maintaining shield height. |
| **`[K]` (Stab)** | Center Thrust | High Guard | Quick poke down the middle; great for stopping approaches. |
| **`[O]` (Special 2)** | 1:30 (High-Right) | Front Guard | **Only front-facing shield push**; safest defensive poke. |

### Practical Sword & Shield Strategy

1.  **Turtle and Poke:** Hold `[O]` (Special 2) to maintain your forward shield guard. Thrust with `[K]` directly out of this stance to pierce incoming rushers.
2.  **Crouch Trap:** Hold `[,]` to sink into a crouched position. Most high attacks will sail harmlessly over your helmet while your shield absorbs midline strikes. Tap `[I]` or `[K]` to strike back from low elevation.

## Weapon Masterclass: Dual Hatchets

Hatchets discard the safety of a broad shield in exchange for blazing attack chains. In this section of our **gladiomori tutorial**, we focus on raw momentum. 

Because hatchet hitboxes are smaller, their guards require precise timing. A passive hatchet fighter is a dead fighter—you must crowd your opponent's space.

| Input Key | Attack Vector | Active Block Direction | Combat Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`[U]` (Special 1)** | Double Swipe (9:00) | Open / Minimal | Swings both axes simultaneously from the left. |
| **`[J]` (Left Strike)** | 9:00 (Left) | Right Guard | Attacks your left while catching counters on your right. |
| **`[I]` (High Strike)** | 12:00 (Overhead) | Right Guard | Downward cleave designed to punish whiffed thrusts. |
| **`[L]` (Right Strike)** | 3:00 (Right) | Left Guard | Quick horizontal hook. |
| **`[O]` (Special 2)** | 2:00 (Upper Right) | High-Left Guard | Off-angle chop that bypasses forward-facing guards. |
| **`[,]` (Low Strike)** | 6:00 (Low Chop) | High-Left Guard | Crouched strike to ankles and kneecaps. |

### The "Medieval Blender" Chain

The defining mechanic of dual hatchets is rapid recovery blending. 
*   **The Loop:** Initiate with `[U]` (Dual Swipe from 9 o'clock), immediately cancel into `[L]` (Right Strike), and follow with `[I]` (High Chop).
*   **The Rule of Opposites:** Attacks from a guard position strike in the **opposite direction** of your current block. To maintain continuous defense while slashing, alternate between left-side and right-side inputs (`[J]` into `[L]`).
*   **Deflecting Thrusts:** While `[K]` is not an actual thrust with hatchets, player reports indicate that tapping `[K]` while circling clockwise (to your right) can deflect enemy spear and sword thrusts harmlessly away.

## Weapon Masterclass: The Bardiche

The Bardiche is a massive polearm offering devastating reach, cleaving capability, and extreme lethality. However, it requires precise mechanical discipline: **only one stance naturally ends in a guard (Low Attack `[,]`)**. Every other swing leaves you exposed if you miss.

### Clock Angles and Arc Geometry

The Bardiche functions on sweeping pendulum arcs rather than quick parries. Understanding its swing continuity lets you chain moves fluidly.

| Input Combo | Swing Trajectory | Tactical Purpose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`[J]` (Left Attack)** | 10:30 down to 4:30 | Broad descending cleave; establishes zone control. |
| **`[U]` > `[J]`** | 11:00 down to 5:00 | Accelerated vertical chop; breaks through weak blocks. |
| **`[I]` (High Attack)** | 12:00 down to 6:00 | Pure vertical overhead; outranges standard swords. |
| **`[L]` (Right Attack)** | 3:00 across to 9:00 | Wide horizontal sweep to catch lateral dodges. |
| **`[J]` > `[L]`** | Rising 4:00 to 10:00 | Uppercut strike; lifts underneath shields. |
| **`[U]` (Special 1)** | Rising 5:00 to 12:00 | Quick chest-height pop; great get-off-me tool. |
| **`[,]` (Low Attack)** | 7:00 Sweep (Crouch) | **Only true guard stance**; defends against low rushes. |

### Essential Bardiche Combos

*   **Vertical Pressure Loop:** Press `[U]` > `[I]` or `[J]`. Because vertical paths share identical recovery planes, your fighter seamlessly resets the head of the axe for repeated overhead chops.
*   **The Wrapped Thrust:** Execute any slash (`[J]` or `[L]`), then immediately input `[K]` (Stab). This wraps the blade around your opponent's active guard, slipping past their weapon into their ribs.
*   **The Windup Poke:** Input `[O]` (Special 2) to pull the axehead backward to your left side, effectively "winding up" the polearm. Release into `[K]` for a long-distance thrust that catches foes off-guard.

## Advanced Survival: Spacing and Neutral Play

Any solid **gladiomori tutorial** must emphasize footwork. Games driven by physics hitboxes reward proper spacing just as much as correct key inputs.

```
       [Zone 1: Bardiche Range] (Maximum Spacing)
                   ↓
     [Zone 2: Sword & Shield Range] (Mid Spacing)
                   ↓
     [Zone 3: Dual Hatchets Range] (Pocket Brawl)
```

1.  **Defanging the Snake:** When facing longer weapons like the Bardiche, don't aim for the head right away. Step into Zone 2 and attack the weapon shaft or outstretched arms. Striking their arms interrupts their attack animations and can disarm or critically wound them.
2.  **The Low Pass Maneuver:** If you are running hatchets against a sword or polearm, use crouched stances (`[,]`) while moving to your right. By ducking under horizontal cleaves, you step past the lethal tip of the weapon and enter Zone 3 where your hatchets dominate.
3.  **Baiting the Whiff:** Tap forward to feign an attack, then immediately backstep while holding a neutral high guard. Once their weapon thuds into the arena floor or slices empty air, release your stored strike.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### What is the most effective weapon in this gladiomori tutorial for complete beginners?
The **Sword and Shield** is the most accessible loadout for beginners. Its attacks feature wide, automatic shield placements, and the `[O]` (Special 2) input provides a secure front-facing guard that keeps you safe while learning enemy swing patterns.

### Why do my attacks feel delayed or sluggish?
Combat is governed by physical momentum and weapon weight rather than canned, instant animations. If you swing a heavy weapon like the Bardiche, your gladiator must physically wind up and complete the swing arc. Chaining compatible attacks (such as vertical strikes into other verticals) dramatically cuts down recovery time.

### Can I play Gladio Mori using a mouse?
No, mouse input is not supported due to the multi-directional stance mechanics and the integrated move editor. You must use directional keyboard inputs or a dual-analog controller to manage strike trajectories and guards.

### How do I defeat opponents who constantly block with shields?
To defeat turtle players, avoid striking their front shield face. Instead, bait out an attack by stepping backward, or use low crouched sweeps (`[,]`) to target unprotected shins. With the Bardiche, you can also wrap stabs (`[K]`) around their shield perimeter after starting a wide horizontal slash.
