## Spatial Awareness and Footwork Strategy

Combat with the **gladiomori katana** is defined as much by your feet as by your arms. Because your defensive blocks lack the absolute protection of a wooden tower shield, you must utilize superior footwork to evade incoming swings.

1. **Maintain Critical Distance (Maai):** Stay slightly outside the tip of your opponent's weapon range. Bait them into starting a commitment animation.
2. **Pivot-Step Countering:** As their blade passes through the apex of its arc, step outward along their weapon arm side. This puts you out of their active damage vector.
3. **Step-Through Decapitations:** Step forward precisely as your horizontal slash initiates, transferring whole-body kinematic weight into the edge.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I stop my katana from bouncing off enemy armor in Gladio Mori?
When using the **gladiomori katana**, hitting rigid plate armor head-on causes edge deflection. To bypass heavy armor, calibrate your custom moves to target joints and soft gaps—such as the groin, armpits, and unarmored neck areas. Slicing with high rotational momentum rather than striking with low-speed contact also significantly reduces deflection chance.

### Can the katana parry heavy weapons like greathammers or two-handed broadswords?
While the blade can deflect heavy attacks, direct parries against massive blunt weapons often inflict high posture shock and can destabilize your stance. Against ultra-heavy weapons, players achieve far better results by using dodging footwork to avoid the impact entirely, then stepping in to punish the recovery window.

### Is thrusting or slashing better with the katana?
Both methods have distinct roles. Slashing produces wider hitboxes and higher bleed damage against light or medium opponents. Thrusting gives you direct forward reach to punish lunges and slips effortlessly past smaller shields into vulnerable torso regions. An optimal custom animation set includes both tools.
