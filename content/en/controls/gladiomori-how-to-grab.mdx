## Understanding Gladio Mori's Grip and Physics System

Unlike conventional fighting games where grabbing is mapped to a single universal button press (like `R1` or light punch plus light kick), *Gladio Mori* operates entirely on a physics-based, keyframe-driven animation engine. In this game, your character model does not rely on canned lock-on animations to hold equipment or interact with opponents. Every limb behaves according to its assigned physical state, joint constraints, and grip toggles configured inside the Move Editor or default character stances.

When players ask about grabbing, they are typically referring to two distinct actions:
1. **Weapon Gripping (Holding):** Setting your primary hand and off-hand to lock onto a weapon's hilt, haft, or pommel.
2. **Dynamic Grappling / Hand Contact:** Using open or closed hands to manipulate an opponent's body, catch their limbs, or wrestle in close quarters.

Because every attack in *Gladio Mori* is essentially a sequence of physical poses running through an internal timeline, your hands will only grasp objects if their state is explicitly defined as active gripping during that timeframe. Checking the [Gladio Mori Steam Community Hub](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2689120) reveals that grip issues are among the most frequent stumbling blocks for newcomers designing custom animations.

| Grip Type | Mechanics Overview | Primary Use Case | Risk Factor |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary One-Hand** | Default hand locked to weapon grip node | Quick thrusts, short swords, rapiers | Higher chance of disarm on heavy impact |
| **Two-Handed (Dual Grip)** | Both hands actively assigned to weapon nodes | Greatswords, polearms, heavy execution strikes | Reduced reach if elbows are over-bent |
| **Dynamic Clinch / Grapple** | Open hand collision with opponent ragdoll joints | Disarming, unbalancing, close-range throws | High vulnerability if counter-struck mid-reach |
| **Half-Swording Grip** | Dominant hand on hilt, off-hand gripping blade | Armored piercing, high-precision control | Limits swing arc and pure slicing momentum |

## Gladiomori How to Grab: Step-by-Step Stance Configuration

The primary reason players run into issues with one-handed swinging when using two-handed weapons comes down to stance configuration. If you do not assign your secondary hand to hold the weapon during the default idle pose, your warrior will spawn holding the blade in only one hand.

To solve this, you need to configure your idle stance and basic movement loop inside the Move Editor. Follow this detailed sequence to fix your grip:

```
[Main Menu] -> [Move Editor / Stance Manager] -> [Select Weapon Profile]
   │
   ├──> [Select Stance: Idle / Neutral]
   │       │
   │       ├──> Select Left Hand Joint -> Set State to "Hold / Grab"
   │       └──> Select Right Hand Joint -> Set State to "Hold / Grab"
   │
   └──> [Snap Off-Hand to Weapon Haft / Grip Point] -> [Save Stance]
```

### 1. Access the Character Stance Setup
Open your custom fighter or moveset editor. Load the specific weapon preset you intend to use (such as the Longsword, Poleaxe, or Katana).

### 2. Configure the Idle Stance Hand States
Navigate to your **Idle Pose** (the baseline pose your gladiator holds when not executing an active attack). Inspect both the dominant and non-dominant hand nodes. According to player experience shared on Steam forums, many players forget that both hands must be individually toggled to "Hold" in the idle stance. If the off-hand is set to "Free" or "Ragdoll," it will swing loosely by your side.

### 3. Anchor Hands to the Weapon Bones
Snap the target hand IK (Inverse Kinematics) point directly onto the designated weapon grip slot. Ensure the grip collision radius overlaps the weapon model cleanly.

| Configuration Step | Setting Target | Recommended Setting | Common Mistake |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Step 1: Stance State** | Hand Grip Property | `Hold` / `Lock to Object` | Leaving secondary hand as `Relaxed` |
| **Step 2: IK Snapping** | Hand Position Bone | Snap to Weapon Haft Bone | Placing hand in air without bone snap |

| **Step 4: Muscle Tension** | Wrist/Elbow Stiffness | Medium-High (65–85%) | Setting to zero, causing limp wrists |

## Setting Up Keyframes for Two-Handed Weapons

Once your idle stance is correctly gripping the weapon with both hands, you must preserve that connection across your combat keyframes. When learning **gladiomori how to grab** throughout a complete swing, understanding timeline inheritance is vital.

If an attack animation transitions from an idle stance into a wind-up, the animation system reads every keyframe for grip persistence. If a keyframe lacks a grip flag on the secondary hand, the off-hand releases immediately, turning your greatsword strike into an awkward, weak one-handed swing.

| Keyframe Phase | Left Hand State | Right Hand State | Kinetic Purpose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Keyframe 0 (Neutral)** | Locked (Hold) | Locked (Hold) | Stabilizes weapon balance and alignment |
| **Keyframe 1 (Wind-up)** | Locked (Hold) | Locked (Hold) | Builds torque across shoulders and torso |
| **Keyframe 2 (Apex / Cock)** | Locked (Hold) | Locked (Hold) | Maximum lever arm potential energy |
| **Keyframe 3 (Impact)** | Locked (Hold) | Locked (Hold) | Transfers mass through rigid collision box |
| **Keyframe 4 (Follow-through)** | Locked or Release* | Locked (Hold) | Optional release for extended single-hand reach |

*\*Note: Community reports indicate that deliberately releasing the off-hand during the late follow-through phase (Keyframe 4) can extend weapon reach by up to 15%, though it temporarily reduces recovery speed.*

### Maintaining Grip Stability Under Physics Torque
In *Gladio Mori*, heavy weapon collisions exert rotational torque back into your character's joints. If your hand's grip constraint has low stiffness, high-speed blade clashes will rip the weapon right out of your fingers. 

Inside the editor:
- Set **Grip Strength** to maximum for primary weapons.
- Set **Joint Rigidity** on forearms to at least 70% to prevent the off-hand from snapping free during parries.
- Always check that your keyframe interpolation curves do not pull the hands further apart than your character's natural wingspan, or the physics engine will forcibly detach one hand.

## In-Match Grappling and Dynamic Grabbing

Beyond holding weapons, players constantly search for **gladiomori how to grab** opponents to disarm, unbalance, or pin them. Dynamic grappling is one of the most technical mechanics in the game because it relies on active ragdoll collision physics rather than predetermined throw cinematics.

```
Approach Angle (Diagonal) ──> Slip Past Weapon Reach ──> Extend Open Hand IK
                                                               │
Disarm Weapon <── Lock Hand Node to Opponent Wrist / Collarbone <┘
```

To execute an effective grab or clinch in live combat:

1. **Create an Unarmed or Free-Hand Stance:** You cannot grab an opponent's body with a hand that is currently locked to a two-handed weapon grip. You must design a stance or move where one hand is free (e.g., using a single-handed arming sword, dagger, or bare fists).
2. **Set Hand Collision to Grab Mode:** In your custom move, configure an open-palm reach keyframe where the hand transitions to active grabbing upon colliding with an enemy limb.
3. **Target High-Leverage Collision Zones:** The best grab targets are the opponent's weapon wrist, neck/collar, and waist. Grabbing an enemy's weapon wrist neutralizes their swing momentum, allowing you to counter with a quick pommel strike or dagger stab.

| Clinch Technique | Target Joint | Tactical Goal | Counter-Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Wrist Control** | Weapon-side Wrist | Disarms opponent or halts forward slash | Quick backward hop or pommel push |
| **Collarbone Pull** | Upper Chest / Neck | Destabilizes balance, setting up sweep | Heavy downward elbow strike |
| **Waist Tackle** | Pelvis / Lower Spine | Drives opponent to the floor for pin | Sprawling back and driving knee upward |
| **Blade Catch (Risky)** | Enemy Weapon Haft | Stops polearm leverage instantly | Blade pull-back, slashing fingers |

## Troubleshooting Common Grabbing Bugs and Grip Glitches

Because *Gladio Mori* combines animation timelines with soft-body ragdoll physics, several common configuration mistakes can break hand interactions. Here is how to diagnose and resolve the most frequent issues.

### The "Only Starting Hand Holds Weapon" Bug
Community reports frequently highlight an issue where a gladiator spawns into a match holding their weapon with only one hand, ignoring the secondary grip. 

* **Cause:** The secondary hand was snapped visually to the weapon in the editor, but the "Hold" toggle was left disabled on the baseline stance, or the hand IK was attached to the world space instead of the weapon bone.
* **Fix:** Re-enter the Move Editor, select your idle stance, click the off-hand joint, and verify that the attachment target is parented directly to the weapon's secondary grip bone.

### Weapon Ejection on Swing Start
Sometimes a fighter begins an attack, and the weapon immediately flies out of their hands before making contact.

* **Cause:** Hand spacing across keyframes exceeds character arm reach. If Keyframe 1 pulls the hands further apart than the arm geometry allows, the physics engine treats the tension as an impossible joint state and breaks the grip constraint.
* **Fix:** Check the distance between your character's hands in the 3D viewport across all frames. Keep the distance between both wrists consistent throughout the swing.

| Symptom | Root Cause | Solution |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Off-hand drops blade immediately | Stance grip flag unset | Set secondary hand to `Hold` on frame 0 of idle stance |
| Weapon vibrates and flies into air | Inverse Kinematics overextended | Shorten reach; pull elbows closer to torso |
| Off-hand misses opponent during clinch | Reach trajectory too narrow | Widen collision box on palm during reach keyframe |
| Blade rotates uncontrollably in hand | Grip stiffness set too low | Increase wrist rotational friction in stance properties |

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### In Gladio Mori how to grab a weapon with both hands if only one hand holds it?
To fix this common issue, open the Move Editor and inspect your baseline **Idle Stance**. Click on your gladiator's off-hand node, set its behavioral state to **Hold**, and snap the hand IK directly onto the weapon's secondary grip bone. Make sure this hold flag is enabled on keyframe 0; otherwise, the game defaults to single-handed mode upon spawning.

### Can you disarm opponents by grabbing their weapons in Gladio Mori?
Yes. If you design a move with an open, free hand that targets an opponent's weapon wrist or haft, the physics collision can interrupt their swing momentum. Combining a wrist grab with a torso pull or sweeping kick creates enough torque to rip the weapon from their grip.

### Why does my character drop their weapon as soon as I swing?
This typically occurs when the keyframes in your swing animation stretch your character's arms beyond their physical length. When the distance between the hands exceeds the character model's anatomical limits, the physics engine forcibly breaks the grip constraint to prevent skeletal distortion. Pull the hand keyframes closer together to fix this.

### Can I change from a two-handed grip to a one-handed grip mid-fight?
Yes. You can program separate move strings within your moveset profile. For instance, you can design an attack sequence that starts with a two-handed overhead strike, releases the off-hand during the follow-through keyframe, and transitions into a one-handed thrust for maximum reach.
