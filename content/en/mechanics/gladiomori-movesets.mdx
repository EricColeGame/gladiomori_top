## Anatomy of Combat Animations in Gladio Mori

In a physics-based simulation, an attack is not merely an uninterruptible script—it is a physical transformation applied to skeletal joints. When you execute an action from your loadout, your gladiator transfers kinetic force through their arms, torso, and legs into the weapon. Because of this, animations dictate reach, strike velocity, recovery balance, and center-of-mass stability.

### The Mechanics of Momentum and Impact

Every custom action mapped to your hotbar features three core phases:

1. **Wind-up (Telegraph):** The initial limb positioning that loads potential energy. A long wind-up makes strikes easier to parry or interrupt but often carries massive end-swing force.
2. **Release & Arc (Active Hitbox):** The physical swing where collisions register damage. Center-of-mass rotation determines whether an attack merely grazes armor or crushes through defenses.
3. **Recovery & Centering:** The motion where the skeleton attempts to return to a neutral stance. Poorly tuned sequences leave the gladiator off-balance and vulnerable to sweeps.

| Combat Phase | Mechanical Influence | Optimal Tactic | Risk Factor |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Wind-up** | Generates weapon speed and telegraphs intent | Feint or initiate from safe spacing | High susceptibility to quick jabs |
| **Impact Window** | Multiplies kinetic force by blade speed | Connect at peak swing trajectory | Over-rotation can deflect off shields |
| **Post-Swing Recovery** | Determines upright stabilization | Cancel momentum into a defensive guard | Extreme stumble if tripped mid-reset |

Understanding these phases is essential when browsing community-crafted animations, as some sequences favor dramatic visual style over functional dueling stability.

```
Light Thrusting Blades  ──> Low Mass   ──> Requires rapid joint snap & short wind-up
```

## Building Your Own Custom Gladiator: Advanced Animation Tips

Creating custom **gladiomori movesets** using internal editing tools allows you to sculpt personal combat choreography. When designing a signature attack sequence, avoid common design pitfalls that look impressive in practice but fail under live physics conditions.

### Balancing Weight Shifts and Limb Trajectories

```
[Hip Rotation] ──> [Shoulder Drive] ──> [Arm Extension] ──> [Impact Target]
      │                                                              ▲
      └── (Must maintain center-of-mass over foot base) ─────────────┘
```

The most frequent mistake in custom animation creation is ignoring foot placement. If an animation commands both arms to snap forward while the torso bends past 45 degrees, ragdoll gravity pulls the gladiator forward into a faceplant. 

To maintain stability:
* **Stagger Foot Steps:** Always script a leading foot forward just before an overhead strike reaches terminal velocity.
* **Keep Guard Arcs Tight:** Defensive moves should keep elbows tucked near the ribcage. Flaring arms outward creates open vulnerabilities for opposing blade tips.
* **Build Counter-Momentum:** Following a wide horizontal slash, script a subtle reverse torso rotation to brake the weapon's angular momentum, enabling faster defensive resets.

## Troubleshooting Common Moveset and Physics Glitches

Custom animation profiles can occasionally cause unexpected ragdoll behavior when collisions interact unpredictably with arena geometry.

### Joint Hyper-Extension and Spaghetti Limbs
If your gladiator’s arms twist or get pinned behind their back following a parry, your animation’s joint velocity likely exceeded the engine's physical constraint thresholds. Lowering the rotation speed in the mod's configuration files solves this issue.

### Inability to Recover from Knockdown
Movesets built purely for high-speed offense often lack proper get-up sequences. Always retain an unedited recovery action to stand upright cleanly if knocked prone by an opponent's blunt strike.

### Phantom Weapon Drops
When weapons swing too fast into static obstacles (such as arena pillars or heavy shields), the sudden kinetic spike can force your character’s hands open. Adding a slight delay to the acceleration curve helps prevent weapon loss upon impact.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are custom gladiomori movesets and how do they work?
In Gladio Mori, movesets are user-created or modded animation scripts that determine how a character moves their body and wields weapons. Because the title operates on ragdoll and joint physics rather than fixed combat animations, these movesets dynamically impact swing speed, strike force, stability, and hit accuracy.

### Can using custom gladiomori movesets get you banned in multiplayer?
Standard community servers generally embrace custom movesets, creative loadouts, and custom map mods. However, some competitive servers restrict certain animation files or enforce vanilla configurations to maintain balanced play. Always check server rules before joining matches with heavily modified animations.

### How do I unlock the "Best offense is offense" achievement with custom setups?
According to community reports, players can easily earn this achievement by equipping a defensive loadout that features a dedicated Shield Slap (often mapped to Action 6 in popular mods) and landing a lethal strike on an opponent using the shield impact rather than a blade.

### Why do some custom gladiomori movesets make my character stumble constantly?
Stumbling occurs when an animation shifts your gladiator's center of mass beyond their foot support base without scripting a compensating step. Heavy weapons amplify this problem. To fix it, use animations with staggered footwork or pair the moveset with lighter weapons to reduce torso pull.
